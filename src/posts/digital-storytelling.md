DLF is pleased to welcome the **2026 Virtual DLF Forum Digital Storytelling Fellows**. This new fellowship experience supports the Forum's new [**Digital Storytelling Presentation** session format](https://www.diglib.org/digital-storytelling-in-practice-a-new-session-format-for-the-dlf-forum/) and centers on emerging technologies and ethical practice across libraries, archives, museums, and related fields.

The DLF Forum is committed to expanding **access, uplifting new voices, and cultivating a diverse community of practice**. Our Fellowship Program is an important part of this commitment, helping reduce financial barriers while creating opportunities for emerging and established professionals to build relationships, exchange ideas, and contribute their perspectives to the DLF community.

We extend our deep gratitude to **MetaArchive Cooperative** for its generous support of the 2026 Fellowship Program. Their contribution helps DLF create meaningful opportunities for Fellows to explore new ways connecting digital storytelling with community engagement.

We are excited to introduce the 2026 Digital Storytelling Fellows and welcome the perspectives, creativity, and experiences they will bring to this year’s Forum.

Mohammad Masudur Rahaman is a Professor of Practice at the Asian University for Women in Bangladesh, where he teaches photography, filmmaking, screenwriting, and digital storytelling. A filmmaker and visual storyteller, he has directed award-winning documentaries and is deeply interested in how storytelling can preserve memory, identity, and community histories. He has developed his digital storytelling practice largely through self-directed learning and currently works with students to build a growing photographic archive of more than 12,000 images documenting Chittagong. His long-term vision is to establish a Digital Storytelling School in Chittagong that connects storytelling, education, community archives, and alternative media.

<details class="fellow-bio">
<summary><span class="fellow-bio-show">Read biography</span><span class="fellow-bio-hide">Hide biography</span><span class="sr-only">: Liang-Chun Wu</span></summary>

Liang-Chun Wu is the Digital Collections Curator at the Center for the Art of East Asia in the Department of Art History at the University of Chicago. Working at the intersection of digital humanities, museum collections, and artificial intelligence, she researches how embeddings and large language models can support digital research infrastructure. At Fantastic Futures 2026, she will present a human-in-the-loop AI pipeline that uses embeddings to surface provenance relationships hidden across dispersed museum collections; she has also presented to museum colleagues on storytelling through 3D models. Drawing on her MA in Digital Studies from the University of Chicago and her computer science training through Georgia Tech’s OMSCS program beginning in 2027, she aims to build responsible AI systems—including agentic deep-research tools—that help scholars and the public explore ambiguity in cultural heritage data without flattening it and tell new stories through digital methods. She welcomes collaborations on AI for cultural heritage, agentic search, digital research infrastructure, and new ways of interpreting and telling stories with cultural collections.
